Erebuz moves a token from one chain to another privately. The sender deposits on the source chain; the recipient receives on the destination chain, in the same token or a different one. Nothing on-chain links the deposit to the payout.

## How a transfer works

A transfer runs in three legs:

1. **Bridge in.** The deposit is bridged, and swapped if needed, into USDC on Arbitrum (the privacy hub). Bridging uses [Relay](https://relay.link) deposit addresses, so the sender just sends funds to an address. No wallet connection, no signing.
2. **Shield and unshield.** The USDC is shielded into a [Railgun](https://railgun.org) pool and unshielded to a fresh address. This is the step that breaks the link between deposit and payout.
3. **Bridge out.** The result is bridged, and swapped if needed, into the destination token and sent to the recipient.

The sender only touches the first leg. Everything after the deposit is handled by the service.

## What is private

An observer sees a deposit into the hub and, separately, a payout out of the Railgun pool. The shielded pool sits between them, so the deposit cannot be tied to the payout, or the sender to the recipient.

The service knows the full route. Privacy is against outside observers, not the operator.

## Fees and the quote

Every transfer is quoted before it runs. The quote is a live estimate refreshed against the market, not a locked rate.

The fee is charged on the token the recipient receives:

- `fee = max($1, 1.5% of the output)`.
- Deposit 100 USDC and ask for ETH: Relay produces about 0.055 ETH, the fee is 1.5% of that ETH, and the remainder is delivered.

Because prices move while a transfer runs, the delivered amount tracks the quote within the fee buffer rather than being fixed to the last decimal.

## Supported assets

Any token pair that Relay can route to and from USDC on Arbitrum works, across every chain Relay covers. List what is available with `GET /api/relay/chains` and `GET /api/relay/tokens`.
